## A Note on Data Verification

Before finalizing any actor's hit count this week, every raw dashboard number was checked against the underlying document type it actually represents. Filtering `src_ip.keyword` alone on the `logstash-*` data view returns every document type recorded for that IP — Honeytrap connections, Suricata alerts, P0f fingerprints, and fourteen others — summed together as a single count. For 193.46.255.112, that unfiltered total came out to 1,430,861, against a true Honeytrap-only count of 119,228 (within normal noise of the dashboard's 119,546). The gap was almost entirely P0f: 926,964 raw P0f records for this one IP, but only 2 distinct OS fingerprints in the highest-volume hour — a roughly 3,400-to-1 ratio of log lines to genuine new observations, consistent with P0f re-logging the same handshake fingerprint on every retry during a connection storm rather than 900,000 real fingerprinting events.

The same spot-check was run against the other two profiled actors (Cowrie for 103.126.161.66, Sentrypeer for 57.131.136.246) and both matched their dashboard totals exactly. The 318-event gap between the dashboard's 119,546 and the direct Honeytrap document count of 119,228 for 193.46.255.112 could not be independently reconciled during this pass — it's small enough to be ordinary indexing lag or a dashboard refresh-timing artifact rather than a sign of further inflation, but it's called out here rather than silently absorbed into "within normal noise." **The headline attack counts in this report are Honeytrap/Cowrie/Sentrypeer-filtered counts, verified against raw record counts — not raw `logstash-*` totals.** P0f and Suricata data are cited below for OS fingerprinting and signature context only, never as additional attack volume.

## Methodology

Each profiled IP and its ASN were checked against OSINT sources — AbuseIPDB, VirusTotal, GreyNoise, Shodan, and Hurricane Electric/RIPE WHOIS and RDAP — for abuse history, host fingerprinting, and vendor detection consensus. Every candidate IP and ASN was also checked against WTIR-01 through WTIR-10 for recurrence before being written up, rather than relying on recognizing a familiar IP from memory.

**Confidence tiers**, assigned per finding rather than per IP:

| Tier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **High** | Directly confirmed by first-hand telemetry (GreyNoise "observed," matching Suricata exploit-classified signatures, RIPE Atlas probe geolocation) or independently corroborated across multiple unrelated sources |
| **Medium** | A plausible, well-supported read of the data, but resting on inference — a shared registrant name, a hostname pattern, a single source's classification |
| **Low** | Suggestive but unverified — a naming convention, a subnet neighbor, a single low-volume signature with no exploit classification |

**Terminology and limitations:** "Attacks" and "events" refer to logged honeypot interactions, not confirmed compromises. A Suricata alert signature with an attached CVE ID means traffic matched a known exploit pattern, not that the exploit succeeded against a real target. GeoIP and registered ownership tell you who administers a network, not who is behind the keyboard.

## The Three Actors We Looked Into

### Investigation 1 — 193.46.255.112 (UNMANAGED LTD, AS47890) | Fifth Consecutive Week, Fortinet Exploitation Sweep

**119,546 hits (verified: 119,228 against Honeytrap) | Rank #1**

![WTIR-11 Threat Actor 1 Romania](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KingMike77/Tpot-Honeynet-Threat-Intelligence/main/Screenshots/week-11/WTIR-11_Threat_Actor_1_Romania_.png)

This is the longest-running recurring actor in the series. First profiled in WTIR-07, it posted 165,061 hits in WTIR-08 (confirmed via RIPE Atlas in Timișoara, Romania) and 163,513 in WTIR-09 — three straight weeks at or near the top of this series. In WTIR-10 the operator switched to a new IP (2.57.121.120) and volume collapsed 93% to just 10,712 hits. This week it is back on its original IP, 193.46.255.112, with volume rebounding to 119,546 — roughly 19% of the week's total honeypot traffic.

Nearly the entire hit count landed on Honeytrap, targeting ports 80, 443, 2087, 2888, and 2929.

![WTIR-11 Threat Actor 1 Attacks by Honeypot Histogram](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KingMike77/Tpot-Honeynet-Threat-Intelligence/main/Screenshots/week-11/WTIR-11_Threat_Actor_1_Attacks_by_Honeypot_Histogram.png)

Suricata's alert-category histogram shows "Misc activity" building through August 23–24, then a sharp single-day spike combining "Generic Protocol Command Decode" and "Attempted Information Leak" on the 25th — followed by near-total silence, the signature of a scripted scan sweep rather than sustained background noise.

![WTIR-11 Threat Actor 1 Suricata Alert Category Histogram](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KingMike77/Tpot-Honeynet-Threat-Intelligence/main/Screenshots/week-11/WTIR-11_Threat_Actor_1_Suricata_Alert_Category_Histogram.png)

Two CVE-2018-133xx signatures tied at 1,253 hits each — the well-documented **CVE-2018-13379** Fortinet SSL VPN pre-auth path-traversal/info-disclosure pair — appear alongside `ET EXPLOIT FortiOS SSL VPN Information Disclosure` and `ET EXPLOIT Fortinet FortiOS/FortiProxy SSL VPN Web Portal Path Traversal`. A `python-requests` User-Agent tagged the traffic throughout, consistent with scripted exploitation tooling rather than manual operation.

![WTIR-11 Threat Actor 1 Suricata CVE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KingMike77/Tpot-Honeynet-Threat-Intelligence/main/Screenshots/week-11/WTIR-11_Threat_Actor_1_Suricata_CVE.png)

**OSINT corroborates this at the highest confidence level of any actor in this report.** RIPE Atlas probes registered under AS47890 physically sit in Timișoara, Romania — an independent network-level confirmation of location, not just a database claim. AbuseIPDB shows **100% confidence**, 1,068 reports from 221 distinct sources, first reported April 30, 2021, most recent report 14 hours old at time of writing. VirusTotal has 12/91 vendors flagging malicious or phishing. GreyNoise classifies the IP malicious (not spoofable, first-hand observed) with an extensive tag list that reads as a checklist of the same commodity edge-device toolkit first tied to this ASN in WTIR-07: FortiOS Info Disclosure CVE-2018-13379, Ivanti Connect Secure Scanner, Ivanti VTM Scanner, Palo Alto PAN-OS CVE-2020-2034 Crawler, Sophos XG Firewall User Portal Scanner, Pulse Secure VPN Scanner, CrushFTP Scanner, FreePBX Crawler, ThinkPHP Framework LFI CVE-2022-47945, WordPress Enumeration, and Masscan Client.

**Assessment — Confidence: High.** Recurring infrastructure observed conducting automated multi-CVE edge-device scanning and exploitation, now confirmed across five consecutive reporting periods and 5+ years of independent abuse history. The single-day, all-or-nothing burst pattern is consistent with a scripted scan sweep rather than continuous background noise. Whether the same human operator has run this infrastructure the whole time, or whether it has changed hands or is shared across tenants, isn't something this data can establish — the recurrence is at the infrastructure level, not confirmed attribution to a single actor.

**Defensive relevance:** This actor's toolkit spans Fortinet, Ivanti, Palo Alto, Sophos, and CrushFTP products, all patched years ago. Organizations running any of these edge devices should confirm patch status against CVE-2018-13379 and the other CVEs in this ASN's tag list specifically, since this traffic shows the exploitation attempts are still live and current, not historical.

### Investigation 2 — 103.126.161.66 (Onebim Vietnam Limited Company, AS135959) | New Actor, Telnet/IoT Credential Stuffing

**12,828 hits (verified: exact match) | Rank #5**

![WTIR-11 Threat Actor 2 Vietnam](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KingMike77/Tpot-Honeynet-Threat-Intelligence/main/Screenshots/week-11/WTIR-11_Threat_Actor_2_Vietnam.png)

Every hit from this IP landed on Cowrie, exclusively on port 23 (Telnet). The attack histogram shows a bell-shaped, roughly four-day campaign window — building from August 23–24, peaking around 500 hits/day, and tapering to zero by August 27 — rather than sustained activity across the full reporting period.

![WTIR-11 Threat Actor 2 Attack Histogram](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KingMike77/Tpot-Honeynet-Threat-Intelligence/main/Screenshots/week-11/WTIR-11_Threat_Actor_2_Attack_Histogram.png)
![WTIR-11 Threat Actor 2 Suricata Alert](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KingMike77/Tpot-Honeynet-Threat-Intelligence/main/Screenshots/week-11/WTIR-11_Threat_Actor_2_Suricata_Alert_.png)

The IP is hosted by Onebim Vietnam Limited Company (AS135959), a small VNNIC-registered ISP/hosting shop based in Ho Chi Minh City, routing through a single upstream peer. Shodan shows a live Linux host running OpenSSH 9.6p1 on Ubuntu, but forward/reverse DNS does not match — the box resolves as `komuz.grudesoaiuru.com` while its mail hostname is `mx2.rugosuslugi.email` — and the surrounding /24 is populated with similarly disposable-looking domains, a pattern consistent with cheap, bulk-provisioned hosting rather than a single dedicated server.

VirusTotal shows 6/91 vendors flagging malicious or phishing (BitDefender, Fortinet, Lionic, CRDF, Chong Lua Dao, G-Data), with several more rated suspicious. OTX AlienVault includes this IP in a community-tagged "Vultr Melbourne Telnet Bruteforce" honeypot list and a broader threat-intel pulse, tagged Scanner / Malicious-IP / Threat-Intel / Blocklist; one analyst comment explicitly cites observation by a T-Pot honeypot network with an OpenCTI threat score of 99/100. GreyNoise has not observed this IP mass-scanning in the past two days — a "selective rather than broad" signal consistent with a short, targeted campaign window rather than continuous internet-wide noise. AbuseIPDB shows **91% confidence**, 493 reports from 50 distinct sources, first reported April 30, 2026, most recent report 2 days old — nearly every report is a tcp/23 port-scan or brute-force, matching the Cowrie/Telnet-only pattern observed here almost exactly.

**Assessment — Confidence: High.** A dedicated Telnet/IoT credential-stuffing host consistent with automated IoT botnet scanning, independently corroborated by multiple external feeds reporting the identical port-23-only behavior over the same general timeframe.

**Defensive relevance:** The exclusive Telnet/23 targeting is a reminder that IoT and embedded devices with factory-default credentials remain a live target class. Organizations with any Telnet-reachable devices — cameras, routers, embedded management interfaces — should confirm Telnet is disabled or restricted, since this kind of credential-stuffing traffic is opportunistic and will find any exposed instance.

### Investigation 3 — 57.131.136.246 (OVH SAS, AS16276) | SIP/VoIP Toll Fraud, Same Pattern as WTIR-01

**5,167 hits (verified: exact match) | Rank #10**

![WTIR-11 Threat Actor 3 France](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KingMike77/Tpot-Honeynet-Threat-Intelligence/main/Screenshots/week-11/WTIR-11_Threat_Actor_3_France.png)

Every hit from this IP went to Sentrypeer on port 5060 (SIP), with activity front-loaded around August 23–24 and declining through the 25th.

![WTIR-11 Threat Actor 3 Histogram](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KingMike77/Tpot-Honeynet-Threat-Intelligence/main/Screenshots/week-11/WTIR-11_Threat_Actor_3_Histogram.png)

All Suricata signatures observed were SURICATA STREAM protocol-anomaly alerts (broken/invalid ACK, SYN resend, RST with no session) — no exploit- or CVE-tagged signatures fired.

The IP is hosted by OVH SAS (AS16276), the same ASN behind this series' very first profiled toll-fraud actor — 54.36.18.72, WTIR-01's Actor 2 — which ran the identical Sentrypeer/SIP-5060 pattern more than three months earlier. RIPE registration for the parent netblock traces to OVH SAS in Roubaix, France, though Shodan and AbuseIPDB geolocate this specific IP to Frankfurt, Germany — a normal artifact of OVH's distributed European data centers rather than a red flag. Shodan shows a Windows-flavored VPS: ports 139 and 445 (SMB v2, authentication enabled) open, plus port 47001 responding with a Microsoft-HTTPAPI/2.0 banner; Shodan also lists CVE-2020-0796 ("SMBGhost") as an associated vulnerability class for that service combination — a note on the host's own exposure rather than evidence of outbound activity. VirusTotal shows only 1/91 vendors flagging malicious (Fortinet), essentially clean. GreyNoise has not observed mass scanning in the past two days, consistent with a short, targeted burst.

**AbuseIPDB is the strongest signal for this actor.** 71% confidence, 240 reports from 12 distinct sources, first reported August 21, 2026 — just before this reporting period — with the most recent report literally seconds old at time of writing. Every recent report is SIP/VoIP brute-force: repeated `SIP INVITE` attempts against an external number, Asterisk `InvalidPassword` security events, and two independent honeypot operators separately logging 5,680 and 7,946 total SIP hits from this same IP.

**Assessment — Confidence: High.** The honeypot telemetry itself demonstrates automated SIP abuse and scanning against port 5060; the toll-fraud interpretation rests primarily on the external corroboration — AbuseIPDB reports and independent honeypot observations describing repeated SIP INVITE attempts against an external number and Asterisk authentication failures — rather than on the honeypot data alone. Taken together, this is consistent with an actively running SIP/VoIP toll-fraud platform on OVH infrastructure, matching the same ASN's toll-fraud role documented at the start of this series in WTIR-01.

**Defensive relevance:** Any exposed SIP/PBX service should require strong authentication and IP allowlisting rather than open internet exposure — the toll-fraud business model depends entirely on finding systems that accept unauthenticated or weakly-authenticated SIP registration, and this actor's INVITE pattern against an external number is the exact mechanism by which victim organizations get billed for calls they never made.

## Lessons and Open Threads

**Aggregate queries need an explicit type filter, every time.** This week's verification pass found that the `logstash-*` data view silently sums 17 different document types when queried without a `type.keyword` filter — the same underlying class of problem as WTIR-06's ConPot GetBulk inflation, but affecting any cross-honeypot aggregate rather than one honeypot's quirky logging. Every headline number in this report was filtered to the specific honeypot it claims to represent and spot-checked against the raw record count before being written down.

**AS47890's toolkit keeps expanding.** What started in WTIR-07 as Fortinet/Palo Alto/Ivanti scanning now carries a GreyNoise tag list spanning at least nine distinct vendor products. Whether this is one operator continuously adding capability or several tenants sharing the same commodity scanning framework isn't something this data can resolve on its own.

**Worth checking next week:** whether 193.46.255.112 holds its rebounded volume or reverts to a new IP as it did in WTIR-10; whether the `zerolimit-servers.cool` family surfaces under a fourth ASN; and whether 107.174.188.218 (AS36352 HostPapa, 25,414 hits, Sentrypeer/SIP 5060) or 46.19.138.10 (AS51852 Private Layer INC, same honeypot/port) resurface next week — both were new this week and hit the identical target profile as each other without a confirmed link.
