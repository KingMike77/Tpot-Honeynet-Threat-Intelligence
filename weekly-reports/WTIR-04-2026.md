WTIR-04-2026 — T-Pot Honeynet Threat Intelligence Report

Reporting Period: July 4 – July 11, 2026
Published: July 12, 2026
Author: Michael Mensah

## Executive Summary

This week the honeynet logged 613,000 attack events, with the top 10 source IPs responsible for roughly half of all traffic. Three actors were profiled in depth. The top source, an Indonesian host, turned out to be a legacy Windows Server 2012 R2 machine with SMBv1 still exposed, independently corroborated by another researcher's honeynet in France. The second, a French-hosted Contabo VPS, was running simultaneous SSH brute-force and VoIP/SIP fraud tooling with a 78% AbuseIPDB confidence score. The most significant finding was the third actor: an Ethiopian IP that traces back to a legitimate academic journal site (jsid.edu.et). The available evidence — a multi-year abuse history, malicious GreyNoise classification, and IoT-style credential-stuffing tags — is consistent with this server having been compromised and repurposed as a botnet node, though external observation alone cannot confirm the underlying cause. A new trend also emerged this week: sustained VNC (port 5901) scanning beginning July 9, worth tracking in the next report. A MITRE ATT&CK technique heatmap for the week is included below.

## What Attackers Were Targeting

![Attacks by Destination Port Histogram showing sustained port 22 and port 5060 activity, a July 8 spike on port 5060, and a new sustained spike on port 5901 beginning July 9](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KingMike77/Tpot-Honeynet-Threat-Intelligence/main/Screenshots/week-04/WITR-04%20Attacks%20by%20Destination%20Port%20Histogram.png)
*Attacks by Destination Port Histogram showing sustained port 22 and port 5060 activity, a July 8 spike on port 5060, and a new sustained spike on port 5901 beginning July 9*

The destination port data tells a clear story about where attacker interest shifted over the course of the week. Port 22 (SSH) held a steady baseline throughout, spiking to roughly 7,800 events on July 10. Port 5060 (SIP) showed recurring moderate activity, peaking near 2,900 events on July 8, consistent with the SIP scanning that Sentrypeer is built to catch. The most notable new development was port 5901 (VNC) — this port was essentially quiet for the first five days of the reporting window, then jumped sharply on July 9 and held steady around 4,000 events per day through July 11. This is a new trend worth watching in next week's report — either a new scanning campaign has picked up our IP specifically, or there has been a broader shift in tooling among the attacker population hitting this honeynet.

![Attacks by Country Histogram showing Indonesia's sustained baseline and France's sharp single-day spike on July 10](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KingMike77/Tpot-Honeynet-Threat-Intelligence/main/Screenshots/week-04/WITR-04%20Attacks%20by%20Country%20Histogram.png)
*Attacks by Country Histogram showing Indonesia's sustained baseline and France's sharp single-day spike on July 10*

Indonesia maintained the highest sustained baseline of attack traffic across nearly the entire week, driven by a single dominant source profiled below. France spiked sharply and briefly on July 9–10, tied to a single high-volume SSH brute-force burst, then dropped back to background levels — a short, intense campaign rather than sustained pressure. The Netherlands, Germany, and the United States showed moderate, fluctuating activity throughout without a single dominant source behind the numbers, consistent with general internet background scanning rather than a targeted campaign.

![P0f OS Distribution pie chart showing an even split between two aged Linux TCP stack fingerprints](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KingMike77/Tpot-Honeynet-Threat-Intelligence/main/Screenshots/week-04/WITR-04%20P0f%20OS%20Distribution.png)
*P0f OS Distribution pie chart showing an even split between two aged Linux TCP stack fingerprints*

P0f, the passive OS fingerprinting tool built into T-Pot, showed the connecting traffic split almost evenly between two fingerprints — "Linux 2.2.x–3.x" and "Linux 2.2.x–3.x (barebones)" — together accounting for the large majority of all fingerprinted connections. A much smaller slice fingerprinted as Windows 7/8, with minor slivers of newer Linux kernels, Windows NT variants, and FreeBSD. The dominance of old/generic Linux TCP stack signatures is typical of lightweight, scripted scanning tools running on cheap VPS instances rather than full-featured modern operating systems — these fingerprints often reflect stripped-down TCP/IP stacks rather than attackers literally running decade-old kernels.

A note on credential telemetry: username/password tagcloud data from Cowrie and Heralding was not pulled from Kibana for this reporting period. That analysis will resume in the next report.

### Actor 1 — 103.16.198.50 | A Legacy Windows Server Hammering RDP-Adjacent Ports
**140,157 hits | Rank #1**

![Kibana dashboard filtered to 103.16.198.50 showing 140k hits exclusively on Honeytrap from PT. Jupiter Jala Arta, Indonesia](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KingMike77/Tpot-Honeynet-Threat-Intelligence/main/Screenshots/week-04/WITR-04%20Threat%20Actor%20from%20Indonesia.png)
*Kibana dashboard filtered to 103.16.198.50 showing 140k hits exclusively on Honeytrap from PT. Jupiter Jala Arta, Indonesia*

Every hit from this IP went to the Honeytrap honeypot, spread across ports 3390, 3391, 3392, 3393, and 3398 — a cluster of non-standard RDP-adjacent ports rather than the standard 3389.

The IP belongs to PT. Jupiter Jala Arta (AS131775), operating under the JALAnet brand out of Jakarta, Indonesia. Shodan-style host fingerprinting shows this is a live Windows Server 2012 R2 machine (hostname FE-02) running IIS 8.5 and Apache 2.4.43/PHP 7.2.32, with a large exposed attack surface: ports 80, 135, 137, 139, 445, 5555, and 5985 all open. Port 445 confirmed SMBv1 still enabled — the same protocol family exploited by EternalBlue and WannaCry — alongside a full stack of Windows RPC endpoints including MS-SAMR, MS-TSCH (Task Scheduler), MS-SCMR (Service Control Manager), and print spooler services. That is a substantial attack surface for a machine that is supposedly the one doing the attacking.

AbuseIPDB showed 4 reports, all filed within the last week, all tagged Brute-Force Web App Attack. All four reports came from the same reporter, "Fasetech," running their own T-Pot sensor — meaning this IP hit an independent researcher's honeynet during the same window it hit ours. GreyNoise has not classified this IP as a mass internet-wide scanner in the past two days, which per GreyNoise's own guidance means it is possibly targeting our infrastructure specifically, or infrastructure similar to it, rather than blindly sweeping the whole internet. Talos rates this IP's Sender IP Reputation as Neutral and it is not currently on any spam blocklists (SpamCop, Spamhaus PBL/SBL, or the Talos block list itself) — consistent with a host being used for scanning/brute-forcing rather than spam or email-based fraud.

**What the data tells us:** This looks like a legacy, poorly-hardened Windows Server — likely compromised or attacker-controlled — being used as launch infrastructure for semi-targeted brute-force campaigns against honeypot-style infrastructure. The corroborating hit against an independent French T-Pot sensor in the same week rules out coincidence.

### Actor 2 — 173.249.34.168 | A Multi-Service Attacker Running SSH and VoIP Fraud Tooling
**41,874 hits | Rank #2**

![Kibana dashboard filtered to 173.249.34.168 showing 42k hits exclusively on Cowrie from Contabo GmbH, France](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KingMike77/Tpot-Honeynet-Threat-Intelligence/main/Screenshots/week-04/WITR-04%20Threat%20Actor%20from%20France.png)
*Kibana dashboard filtered to 173.249.34.168 showing 42k hits exclusively on Cowrie from Contabo GmbH, France*

Every hit from this IP went to Cowrie on port 22 (SSH), and this IP was responsible for the sharp single-day spike that briefly made France the top attacking country of the week on July 10.

The IP is hosted by Contabo GmbH (AS51167), a widely used budget VPS provider, geolocated to Lauterbourg in France despite the hostname (m10268.contaboserver.net) pointing to Contabo's Frankfurt infrastructure. AbuseIPDB rates this IP at 78% Confidence of Abuse with 44 reports from 17 distinct sources in under two weeks. The category tags are the interesting part: SSH Port Scan, Brute-Force, Port Scan, and — critically — Fraud VoIP Port Scan Brute-Force SSH. Shodan-style fingerprinting confirms why: alongside Apache 2.4.58 on port 80, this host is running Asterisk PBX 13.38.3 exposed on port 5060/UDP. This is not a single-purpose SSH brute-forcer — it is a multi-service attack platform running both SSH credential stuffing and VoIP/SIP scanning tooling from the same box.

Reports show this IP hitting honeypot and firewall sensors in Canada, Germany, the UK, the US, and Australia within the same week — a genuinely broad, simultaneous multi-country campaign, not an isolated incident. Notably, the same "Fasetech" T-Pot reporter who flagged Actor 1 also flagged this IP, with an identical Brute-Force Web App Attack signature — meaning both of this week's top two sources hit the same independent honeynet in parallel with ours. GreyNoise has not classified this IP as a mass internet-wide scanner either, again suggesting more selective targeting behavior. Talos rates the Sender IP Reputation as Neutral and it is not currently listed on SpamCop, Spamhaus, or the Talos block list — this IP's abuse is entirely scanning/brute-force based rather than spam or phishing. Worth noting for context: Contabo's broader netblock hosts a real mix of legitimate customer VMs (mail servers, personal sites) alongside at least one outlier — a host spoofing the hostname "testtoolkitshop.coca-cola.com" with a DNS mismatch and a Poor reputation — a reminder that shared budget hosting blocks mix legitimate tenants with clearly fraudulent ones.

**What the data tells us:** This is a confirmed, actively malicious, multi-service attack platform — SSH brute forcing and VoIP/SIP fraud tooling running side by side — with a well-documented abuse history spanning at least six countries in the current reporting window alone.

### Actor 3 — 196.189.91.218 | A Compromised Academic Server Running an IoT Botnet
**35,209 hits | Rank #3**

![Kibana dashboard filtered to 196.189.91.218 showing 35k hits, almost entirely on Cowrie, from Ethio Telecom, Ethiopia](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KingMike77/Tpot-Honeynet-Threat-Intelligence/main/Screenshots/week-04/WITR-04%20Threat%20Actor%20from%20Ethiopia.png)
*Kibana dashboard filtered to 196.189.91.218 showing 35k hits, almost entirely on Cowrie, from Ethio Telecom, Ethiopia*

Nearly every hit from this IP went to Cowrie on port 22, with a single incidental hit against Honeytrap.

The IP belongs to Ethio Telecom (AS24757), Ethiopia's national ISP, and is the most well-documented source profiled this week — 906 AbuseIPDB reports from 170 distinct sources dating back to March 2023, making this a chronic, multi-year abusive host rather than a new or short-lived threat. GreyNoise explicitly classifies this IP as malicious — not merely "unknown," as with the other two actors this week — and confirms the traffic is not spoofed. GreyNoise's tags describe the actual behavior: SSH Connection Attempt, SSH Bruteforcer, SSH Alternative Port Crawler, Generic IoT Default Password Attempt, and Telnet Protocol activity. That combination is a classic Mirai-style IoT credential-stuffing signature — trying default IoT usernames and passwords across both SSH and Telnet — rather than a single targeted SSH campaign.

The most notable finding came from host fingerprinting: the actual machine sitting at this IP is jsid.edu.et — the Journal of Science and Inclusive Development, a real, functioning academic journal site running Open Journal Systems on Apache 2.4.57/Ubuntu, complete with a valid Let's Encrypt certificate current through September 2026. This is not attacker-owned infrastructure. It is a legitimate educational publishing platform. Talos rates this IP's Sender IP Reputation as Poor — notably worse than either of the other two actors this week — though Talos block list status for this specific IP was unavailable at lookup time. Looking at the surrounding Ethio Telecom netblock (196.189.91.x) reinforces the picture: every neighboring IP checked in that range also carries a Poor email reputation, suggesting this isn't an isolated bad actor but a pattern across a wider slice of this ISP's address space — consistent with a broader compromise or abuse problem within that network rather than one single hijacked machine.

**What the data tells us:** This is the strongest finding of the week. The available evidence is consistent with a legitimate academic server being compromised and repurposed for credential-stuffing activity, though external observation alone cannot confirm the underlying cause — a honeynet can show what traffic arrives and where it traces back to, but it cannot confirm what is actually running on the remote machine or how it got there. If this hypothesis is correct, the server would be continuing to serve its legitimate front-end content to visitors while running brute-force campaigns against SSH and Telnet targets in the background, almost certainly without its operator's knowledge. Either way, this is a notable data point: real infrastructure tied to an educational institution, rather than dedicated attacker-owned hosting, showing a multi-year pattern of malicious activity. The Poor email reputation shared across the surrounding netblock suggests this pattern may extend beyond this single server.

## What This Week's Data Suggests

Legacy protocols on internet-facing servers remain a real problem. Actor 1's host still has SMBv1 enabled alongside a fully exposed Windows RPC surface. Nearly a decade after EternalBlue, this pattern keeps showing up on scanned and attacking infrastructure alike.

VoIP and SSH attacks are increasingly running from the same infrastructure. Actor 2 wasn't a single-purpose SSH brute-forcer — it was running Asterisk PBX alongside its SSH campaign, and AbuseIPDB's own category tags reflect that convergence between VoIP fraud tooling and traditional brute forcing.

Compromised legitimate infrastructure may be doing quiet, long-term damage. Actor 3 is the clearest candidate seen in this honeynet to date — a real academic institution's publishing platform, still serving legitimate traffic, appearing alongside IoT-style credential stuffing against unrelated targets over more than three years. Talos data showing Poor email reputation across the entire surrounding Ethio Telecom netblock, not just this one IP, suggests this may not be an isolated case but a wider pattern within that address space. Without direct access to the machine, this remains an inference rather than a confirmed compromise — but it's a good reminder that not every malicious IP is dedicated attacker infrastructure; some of the most persistent sources may be legitimate systems nobody is actively monitoring.

VNC scanning is a new and growing trend on this honeynet. The sustained spike on port 5901 beginning July 9 is worth tracking closely in next week's report to see whether it continues or was a one-off campaign.

Independent honeynet correlation adds real confidence. Both of this week's top two actors were independently flagged by another T-Pot operator running sensors in France, corroborating that these are active, roaming attack sources rather than isolated hits against this honeynet alone.
